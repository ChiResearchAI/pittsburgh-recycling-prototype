# Pittsburgh Recycling — Resident Flow Prototype

A working prototype of the resident-facing flow for City of Pittsburgh
**RFP #2026-RFP-168, Recycling Engagement and Analytics Application**, built by
ChiResearch Technologies, LLC.

**View it:** `https://chiresearchai.github.io/pittsburgh-recycling-prototype/`

## What this is

One flow, working: enter an address and get your collection day and the correct **set-out
location**; search an item and get Pittsburgh's answer with the reason and what to do next.
Every result opens a "Why this answer" panel showing the rule, its owner and its review date.

It demonstrates three things the written proposal argues:

1. **The rear and side exception set is first-class data.** Roughly a fifth of Pittsburgh
   addresses set out at the rear or side rather than the curb. Compare the Troy Hill and
   Beechview samples against the curb-collected ones.
2. **Answers are Pittsburgh's, not a national list.** Glass is not accepted in the curbside
   programme here, and the prototype says so and routes to drop-off instead.
3. **Every answer traces to a human-authored rule** — never generated by a model, per the
   City's answer to Question 20.

## What this is not

**A prototype, not a deployed system.** It is not in use by any municipality. It was built for
this proposal to show the shape of the resident flow and that ChiResearch can build it.

The collection records and material rules are an **illustrative sample** — five addresses and
twelve materials — not the City's production data. In delivery, ChiResearch supplies a base
materials catalogue that Environmental Services co-develops from there, which is the
arrangement the City described at Question 55.

The administrative console, the notification and geo-targeting engine, the analytics views and
the WCAG 2.1 AA conformance work are described in the Response to Scope and priced in the Cost
Proposal. They are not in this page.

## How it runs

A single self-contained HTML file. No backend, no build step, no external dependencies, no
accounts and no resident data collected — consistent with the City's answers at Questions 46
and 61.
